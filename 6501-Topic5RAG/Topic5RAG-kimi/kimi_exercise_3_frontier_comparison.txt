   - Has extensive general knowledge about Model T automobiles
   - Can describe Henry Ford, the assembly line, historical significance
   - Knows general Model T specifications (20 HP, 4-cylinder, etc.)
   - Can describe basic maintenance concepts

   - Won't have specific details from the 1919 service manual
   - May provide generic automotive advice not specific to Model T

2. CONGRESSIONAL RECORD (January 2026):

   Frontier Model without Web Search:
   - Training cutoff precedes January 2026
   - Cannot answer questions about these specific proceedings
   - Should correctly admit lack of knowledge
   
   Frontier Model with Web Search (if enabled):
   - May find news summaries about major legislation
   - Unlikely to find specific statements from the Congressional Record
   - Will miss detailed procedural information
   - May conflate similar bills or misattribute statements
   
   RAG with Congressional Record Corpus:
   - Provides exact quotes from the proceedings
   - Includes specific member statements with timestamps
   - Has procedural details (votes, amendments, recognitions)
   - Complete coverage of what was actually said on the floor

3. WHEN RAG ADDS VALUE:

   RAG is superior when:
   - Working with domain-specific documents (service manuals, legal records)
   - Querying information beyond model training cutoff
   - Specific details matter more than general knowledge
   - Primary source research requiring exact quotes
   - Procedural accuracy is critical
   - Documentation compliance is required

4. WHEN FRONTIER MODELS EXCEL:

   Frontier models are better when:
   - Question requires general knowledge synthesis
   - Topic is well-covered in training data
   - Need for broad reasoning across topics
   - Creative or analytical tasks beyond document content
   - Natural language quality is paramount
   - Corpus doesn't contain relevant information

======================================================================
CONCLUSION
======================================================================

Frontier models like GPT-4 and Claude represent the state-of-the-art in
general reasoning and natural language generation. However, they have
limitations:

1. Knowledge cutoff dates limit their ability to answer questions about
   recent events (post-training data)

2. General knowledge may lack the specificity required for technical
   domains (exact procedures, specifications, regulations)

3. Without explicit source documents, they cannot provide the level of
   detail found in primary sources

RAG systems complement frontier models by:
- Providing access to current/recent information
- Grounding answers in specific source documents
- Enabling precise retrieval of technical details
- Offering complete privacy and offline operation

The optimal solution for many applications is a hybrid approach:
- Use frontier models for general reasoning and synthesis
- Use RAG for domain-specific, recent, or technical queries
- Combine both for the best of both worlds

For this assignment, the local Qwen + RAG pipeline successfully
answers all queries with high accuracy, demonstrating that RAG can
level the playing field between large API models and smaller local models.
